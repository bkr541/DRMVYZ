# EVERYWHERE

EVERYWHERE is a dense, dark CRT installation. The approved composition places a nearly frontal hero occupying roughly one third of the 16:9 width and one half of its height. Larger angled consoles surround it at the upper-left, upper-right and lower corners; smaller monitors fill recessed openings. The installation is authored in physical XYZ coordinates (+Z faces the camera), with 101 independently staged cabinets including extended lateral and far-camera perimeter sets. A curved dark backing sits behind the deepest cabinets. There is no reference-image billboard, runtime placement grid, camera-distance scaling or replacement renderer. The central 16:9 composition retains intentional gaps and natural perspective. The outer wing continues beyond the default frame; portrait crops naturally, and ultrawide up to 32:9 reveals more physical sets. The camera remains at [0,0,14], 38 degrees vertical FOV, with its existing saved offsets and authored distance control; 12.3–15-unit camera safety envelope and 36–39-degree lens limits keep the wall continuous even with saved offsets.

Four cabinet families retain independent aperture and rear profiles: squared broadcast monitor with recessed bottom rail and tuning dial, wood console with side control bank, rounded portable with handle, and deep molded CRT with lower chin. Layered trim, physically tapered housings, grille and ventilation detail are batched by family/material. Convex screen meshes use family-specific transforms and view-normal reflection with soft cyan/amber highlights. Preset-local cabinet shading uses filtered procedural wear, roughness variation, picture-power-dependent narrow bevel highlights, six bounded point lights and a six-neighbor-per-cabinet indirect irradiance approximation. Darkened front planes, directional grazing lights and cavity attenuation replace broad gray face illumination. The backing has no emissive screen content. The default built-in source is the existing eye asset; smaller CRTs use independent crop, a remapped smaller iris, neutral eye grading and curved resting eyelids, rather than a second photo. Eye motion and custom media remain independent. Local bounce is an authored approximation, not physically traced global illumination. No new runtime assets are added.

Screen Coverage defaults to .96 (previous .86), while Cabinet, Cool Rim and Warm Rim palette defaults are adjusted to the reference's dark neutral surfaces, restrained cyan and warm amber. Saved settings and all parameter IDs remain compatible. Existing shared HDR bloom and finish stay in place. All twelve screen programs use physical position and a bounded depth-aware program domain so outer wings participate rather than being excluded by the old coordinate range. Signal Relay is an inward return pulse, distinct from Outward Pulse. The existing shared trigger, media-pool and audio infrastructure is unchanged.

Geometry acceptance checks use actual merged cabinet bounds, the convex glass extent, sampled Cabinet Angle clearance, hero size and tapered carcass ray coverage across aspect ratios and camera distances. The actual tapered shell proxy excludes empty OBB space. These checks are not visual acceptance. The production browser script captures the actual renderer, verifies a luminous central screen and tests exposed backing, camera, lighting, screen programs and source integration. Human comparison against the approved reference remains required before promoting the catalog classification. This reconstruction's development environment lacked installed dependencies and registry access, so actual WebGL appearance could not be inspected here.

## Patterns and timing

| All Eyes | The hero and surrounding monitors hold a clear default signal, with slight exposure differences across the wall. |
| Signal Sweep | A broad band progresses from left to right across the cabinets. |

| Retro Broadcast | Bar changes, warm phosphor color, stronger scanlines, horizontal channel banks. |
| Tape Tracking | Four-beat channel changes, VHS tracking band, snare-driven tearing and color drift, vertical channel banks. |

The canonical Pattern Change toggle starts off. Trigger offers the shared eleven choices and is visible when the saved toggle is on. Manual selection takes effect immediately, leaves Pattern Change at its saved setting and ignores the event already present on the edit frame. Auto Performance uses the shared Mood Conductor's effective choices while the music has a beat grid. Music never edits the manually assigned media, named pool contents, manual Pattern, colors or saved toggle. Build and drop envelopes modulate the current pattern.

BPM Sync uses the shared beat clock when host and preset sync are enabled. With sync off, picture programs use the clock's 120 BPM reference rate. Pattern triggers retain their musical identities. Idle has a stable readable image. Playing music brings progressive exposure motion. Pause holds motion and audio accents. Track changes, seeks, context restoration and re-entry reset transient state using shared transport sources.

## Default eye choreography

Every screen borrows the original eye texture; a preset-local shader displaces only the iris within a fixed eyelid aperture, reconstructs newly exposed sclera and warps actual eyelid pixels into a curved blink. The pose follows the existing pattern phase (no extra clock) and the same kick, snare, build, drop and vocal envelopes. All Eyes synchronizes left/right gazes, blinks and periodic upward rolls; Signal Sweep staggers blinks along X; Outward Pulse and Signal Relay propagate gaze from the centre; Cross Cut uses opposing gaze directions; Channel Hold and Channel Mosaic alternate groups; Ghost Feed and Midnight Replay use staggered blinks and rolls; Static Switch, Retro Broadcast and Tape Tracking have different gaze and blink cadences. Drops roll all eyes upward; builds converge toward a raised gaze. Motion Amount governs intensity, and paused playback holds the exact pose. Track/seek reset follows the existing pattern phase reset. The effect is a texture-based approximation rather than a rigged anatomical 3D eye. The eye is exclusive to the built-in source mode; selecting a single feed or pool suppresses it even during texture decoding; the original CRT effects, named pool selection and user media transforms are preserved. Span mode retains the existing physical mapping at standard aspect ratios and expands the horizontal domain for ultrawide outputs.

The shared Mood Conductor increases Screen Coverage by up to 0.18 on activity and reduces it by up to 0.47 in silence (baseline 0.96, range 0.18–1). Brightness can gain 0.20 on activity (baseline 1.06, range 0–1.8). Peak adds up to 0.22 Bloom, silence subtracts up to 0.20 (baseline 0.34, range 0–1.2). All drives scale with Motion Amount. Shared Audio Intelligence publishes signals, Visual Director publishes build/drop meaning, Choreography routes bounded contributions, and the native renderer consumes them. Parameter State and the workspace session own authored state.

The left Source tab uses the existing Cinema 2.0 Media Source panel and Media Library browser. It accepts images, video and SVG. Selecting an item replaces the default eye feed through the existing media slot. The same media library supports search, collection/library navigation, and opening Media Manager. The native CRT material borrows the shared GPU texture and preserves its presentation. With an unassigned or cleared slot the packaged eye feed returns.

## Named media pools

Use Source → Media Library → Pools to create a named pool and Set Active. Click or right-click a compatible media card and choose Add to Pool → pool name. These are the same persisted pools and active-pool selection used by CANVAS, not a duplicate store. Activating a pool clears the manual screen feed and invalidates outstanding single-source requests; Make Active clears the active-pool selection and assigns that one asset. Deactivating the pool restores the built-in eyes, never the previous individual asset. Reset to Default Eyes clears both assignments without deleting library items. Images, SVG and videos are eligible; text entries are not rendered as CRT media. The pool resolves eligible items from the shared loaded library cache; if some saved items are not yet loaded, browse/load those items in the Media Library. Signed URLs are prepared only for selected channels.

The new Pool Trigger control defaults to Program cadence (table above; legacy programs use four bars). Override it with any of the existing eleven musical triggers when Auto Performance is off. Pool Trigger is independent of Pattern Change. Pattern changes and trigger changes do not consume the already-present event. The pool advances only on unique shared musical event identities during playback, holds during pause or zero motion, and holds in conductor silence with Auto Performance enabled. No timer, local detector, or second beat clock is introduced. Choreographed and repeat modes distribute up to three shared feeds across whole individual CRTs; Span the wall intentionally uses one common feed across the wall. For a larger pool, one channel is replaced per accepted event in pool order. A slow replacement retains the previous ready feed and coalesces intervening events. An active empty pool or failed initial feed displays dark CRT glass instead of bringing back eyes. The three transient media slots use the canonical media loader, video pause/loop and texture lifecycle inside the module's tracked resource scope; they are not serialized as authored state.

## Continuation / integration details

EVERYWHERE owns its own stable scene graph and capabilities. It does not inherit the Corridor preset's schema or rendering descriptions. The manifest IDs (`everywhere-root`, `everywhere-passage`, `everywhere-aim`, `everywhere-world`, `everywhere-camera`, `everywhere-cassettes`) are unchanged for saved presets.

The CRT screen shader composites transparent image/video/SVG pixels against dark CRT glass; eye sampling is gated by exclusive source mode. Contain is the default for logos and pools. Glass edges are anti-aliased with transparent depth-tested convex meshes. The performance controller treats audio timeline revisions as discontinuities, resetting its phase and transient pattern state without editing saved controls.

The preset stays classified as a **reference** in the first-party catalog until a human approves its actual visual output. The existing `scripts/verify-cinema2-everywhere.mjs` is the intended browser/WebGL acceptance check; run `npm run test:cinema2-everywhere` and `npm run test:e2e:cinema2-everywhere` in the application repository with installed dependencies and a Playwright browser. Its output captures the twelve screen programs, playback and drop behavior, portrait framing, Inspector and Source library selection.

## Screen content and camera choreography

The twelve existing pattern IDs now also author per-screen scale, translation, rotation, signal distortion, vertical compression, power and pool channel via instanced attributes. Choreographed Distribution applies full independent transforms; Repeat uses restrained content motion; Span preserves continuous wall-space mapping while retaining power choreography. Preserve Hero Readability protects the central feed. Motion uses the same phase and musical envelopes as the default eyes; no separate media clock or per-television decoder is introduced.

The shared camera runtime supplies drift, tempo sway, lens breathing, smoothing and safety. Camera Motion, Camera Mode, Distance, Sway, Drop Impact, Roll, Zoom on Kick and Follow Screen Choreography are under Design / Camera. The shared choreography routes builds, drops, phrases and snare accents to real camera targets. Camera profiles are selected by the saved pattern (the shared rule conditions use authored parameters); automatic pattern changes still receive the common cinematic camera accents. The camera remains frontal and bounded, never orbits behind the wall. The wall no longer simulates movement by translating or rolling its entire geometry. Cabinet counts, camera envelopes and rendering performance should be visually benchmarked in the browser acceptance run.

EV-02 inspector presentation: Cabinets, Screen Media, Camera and Pattern use the standard nested disclosure under Design, in that order. Camera and Effects / CRT Finish start collapsed; the other Design subgroups start open. Camera Distance retains its saved offset slider (default 14, step 0.1); the preset-local camera safety envelope now limits effective travel to 12.3–15 scene units. It edits only Z of the existing `everywhere-camera-distance` offset vector (default `[0,0,0]`, bounds −3 to 3). Previously saved X/Y offsets remain untouched. Auto Performance retains the existing lock and saved-value ownership. No parameter IDs, native bindings or shared choreography have changed. Cabinet Angle retains its existing 0–1.25 value range but maps to bounded architectural articulation (.65–1.0875× the authored orientations); zero no longer flattens the wall.

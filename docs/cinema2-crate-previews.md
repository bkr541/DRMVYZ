# Cinema 2.0 crate previews

The Cinema 2.0 crate presets use the owner’s `3D Cinema 2.0 Crate` files. The source GLBs live in `public/cinema2/models/crate/`. Glyph Sphere and Wireframe Portal each have their own performance, described below. Paradox Weave’s Parallax Cipher uses an authored camera move. Beam Lattice, Bass Sigil and Moire Orb have their own performances described below.

| Preset | Source GLB | Appearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moire Orb | `geometry_texture_effect_spheres.glb` | Original translucent core in Resonance Reactor, with 48 ring segments and 12 moving fixtures |
| Beam Lattice | `lasers_-_vj.glb` | Reference yellow laser field, seven native bank programs, shared choreography and Auto/Mood |
| Wireframe Portal | `lightweight.glb` | Corner Truss as black steel: gunmetal frames in hard cold and red rim light, hot amber joint cores, cold edge runs, haze, dust, shallow depth of field, a low omnidirectional camera, musical choreography and shared Auto Performance |
| Glyph Sphere | `oyes.glb` | Glyph Armillary: a glowing glyph core inside three tilted glyph bands in ruled ring frames, with beat-chased ticks, a drifting starfield, an omnidirectional camera, musical choreography and shared Auto Performance |
| Paradox Weave | `paradox_abstract_art_of_python.glb` | Parallax Cipher, with staggered angular plates and 3D lettering that aligns from a single viewpoint |
| Bass Sigil | `skrillex_-_vj_-_flash_warning.glb` | The original cellular sphere, particle fields and glow around **a 3D model you choose** (the supplied logo is removed), with eight native programs, musical choreography and shared Auto Performance |

The stable preset IDs are `drmvyz.cinema2.crate-<slug>`. The crate manifests use the shared Three scene module for asset loading, material ownership, and rendering. The original environment pass assembled private scene objects over the shared GLB geometry and textures, with fixed framing and static bloom/color finishing. The two remaining static environments are listed below.

The Paradox file requires the older `KHR_materials_pbrSpecularGlossiness` extension. A small GLTFLoader plugin maps its diffuse/specular/gloss factors to physical material properties during decoding, avoiding the loader's white fallback. This supports factor-based legacy materials; specular/glossiness textures remain rejected by both the asset validator and loader. Source bytes remain untouched.

Every source embeds CC BY 4.0 author, title and source metadata. Credits are retained in the asset records and generated `public/cinema2/attributions.json`. The installer asset budget increases from 132 to 160 MiB to accommodate the supplied files without compression or quality substitutions; the complete shipped asset inventory is 152.15 MiB. Individual file, triangle, texture and GPU limits are unchanged.

Initial crate validation: the three new crate tests verify native compilation/registration, the absence of controls and choreography, byte-for-byte file hashes, and legacy diffuse/gloss decoding. The eighteen shared Three scene tests, asset pipeline tests, TypeScript, focused ESLint and asset inventory check passed. All eight thumbnails were generated through the production runtime and visually reviewed in ignored `artifacts/cinema2-crate/production.png`. Thumbnails live in `public/preset-artwork/`.

The source GLBs contain no cameras or external viewer lighting. The two static environments use authored fixed cameras to show their expanded geometry.

## Paradox Weave — Parallax Cipher

Type the word under **Design → Typography**, in SAY IT's two-input style: **Line Mode** (One Line or Two Lines), **Line 1** and **Line 2** (12 characters each, 20 in all), then **Line Hold** and **Letter Motion**. It starts at `PARADOX` and uses SAY IT's beveled glyph assets and layout. Only one line is on stage at a time, so the word always keeps reading from the reveal camera; with both lines typed they take turns every Line Hold bars (1 to 16, default 4). Line 1 keeps the id `parallax-cipher-text`, so saved settings still load. Editing the line on stage changes it at once with no transition. Paradox Weave also uses the shared Text Library (the TEXT tab): saved scenes and pools hold Line Mode, Line 1 and Line 2, with the same 12/20-character limits as SAY IT. Migration `0059_cinema2_paradox_text_scenes.sql` allows its preset id in the saved-text and pool tables.

The local development preview can use Chopsic by Chequered Ink for the beveled lettering. Generate the local mesh and metrics from the supplied TTF with `node scripts/cinema2-assets/generate-say-it-glyphs.mjs artifacts/parallax-cipher/chopsic.glb artifacts/parallax-cipher/chopsic-metrics.json /path/to/Chopsic-K6Dp.ttf`. The optional assets stay in the ignored workspace for personal, noncommercial preview. The shipped fallback is Anton. [Chopsic by Chequered Ink](https://chequered.ink/product/chopsic/)

The original weave and red accent sit behind the lettering. Staggered banks of the source plates fill both sides of the scene. The 24-second camera path holds the reveal, moves to oblique viewpoints, and returns to the reveal. Plate banks countertwist and the glyphs turn as the view moves. Plate and letter motion follows the shared beat clock (see Controls below). The cyan and magenta near and far contours, letter bevels, registration dots, and red marker are named lighting families.

**Controls (revision 3).** Under **Master Controls**: **BPM Sync** (locks the plate and letter motion to the track tempo, or a fixed 120 BPM when off), **Intensity**, **Audio Reactivity** and **Auto Performance** (off by default). Under **Design → Pattern**: **Pattern**, **Pattern Change** and **Trigger** with six patterns (Reading Line, Open Gate, Collapse, Counter Spiral, Cascade, Shatter), using the shared Mainframe pattern controller and trigger list; a manual pick takes effect at once and a Trigger-driven change eases in over two beats. Under **Design → Geometry**: **Plate Spread**, **Bank Twist**, **Weave Scale** and **Letter Depth**. Under **Design → Lighting**: **Near Contours**, **Far Contours**, **Letter Bevels**, **Registration Dots** and **Red Marker**. **Bloom** is under **Effects**, and five palette colors (**Left Bank**, **Right Bank**, **Letters**, **Weave**, **Red Marker**) are under **Palette**. Every material's authored color is scaled by chosen / authored, so the defaults reproduce the original look. The word stays readable at the camera's reveal hold in every pattern; Shatter throws the letters off the line for a fraction of each beat and they settle back.

**Audio and choreography.** Every route targets one of the preset's own controls (so the Inspector shows each moving and Auto Performance locks it) and scales with **Audio Reactivity**; at 0 the music moves nothing. Continuous: bass widens the walls and swells the weave, the mids turn the banks, the highs light the near and far rim lines, overall energy lifts Intensity, and vocal presence lights the letter bevels. Events: a kick swells the weave and flashes the red marker, a snare flashes the registration dots and the bevels, a drop lifts Bloom, Plate Spread and Intensity. With **Auto Performance** on, the shared Mood Conductor also moves the controls (calm relaxes the twist and dims the near lines, a build pulls the weave in and lights the far banks, a peak lights everything, a shock flares the bevels, silence fades every light) and the shared coverage step drives any remaining numeric control.

**Pattern authority.** Manual Pattern selection sets the base Pattern; the effective Trigger (the saved one, or the music's while Auto Performance is on) may advance it while Pattern Change is on; Auto Performance may substitute **Collapse** for a build and **Shatter** for a drop (and, with Pattern Change off, a pattern chosen by the passage's feel on musical boundaries); the base resumes afterwards. The saved Pattern, Pattern Change and Trigger are never written by automation, a manual Pattern edit is never undone by an event already present, and turning Auto Performance off restores the saved values at once.

**Camera (revision 3).** **Camera Mode** under **Design → Camera** has three modes: **Reveal Hold** (the single sightline where the word reads, at every moment), **Immersive Path** (the default: the authored 24-second swing out and back) and **Manual View** (an orbit around the weave starting from the same view, with **Camera Heading** ±45°, **Camera Elevation** −15° to 30° and **Camera Distance** 10 to 18, shown only in this mode). **View Motion Speed** (Immersive Path only) scales the travel, and zero holds the view. With **BPM Sync** on the path runs at the track tempo divided by 120 times its authored speed (rate clamped to 0.5 to 1.75; the loop is 12 bars at 120 BPM), and at its authored speed with it off. **Zoom on Kick** (Master Controls, on by default) punches the lens in about 1.8° on every kick; only the field of view changes, so the letters stay aligned. No sway, bob or roll is added to the camera, because they would pull the letters off their sightline. A safety box keeps every view, Manual View included, outside the nearest plate banks. The letters' recoil and the banks' follow-through are driven by the camera that is actually drawing, in every mode and at every speed, so Manual View and Reveal Hold behave consistently.

The thumbnail is regenerated through the production runtime (`scripts/generate-preset-artwork.mjs`) and shows the reveal hold.

## Bass Sigil: your own 3D model in the middle

The artist logo that used to sit in the middle of Bass Sigil is removed from the application (its nodes are taken out when the scene is built; the supplied GLB is unchanged, and the sphere, particles and a soft glow remain). The middle now holds **one of your saved 3D models**, generated or imported.

- **Choosing it.** The left **Source** tab lists your 3D models (search, grid or list, the same layout the Canvas Engine uses for media). Clicking one pins its current version in the preset (`user-model:<id>:<version>`); **Remove model** clears it, and with no model the middle is empty. If you later edit the model and save a new version, the tab offers **Use vN** instead of changing the preset silently. A deleted or unavailable model shows why and falls back to empty. Any preset whose scene module declares a model slot gets the same Source tab (the 3D preview presets do too).
- **Controls.** The old Logo controls are now **Model** controls: Model Spin Rate, Sway, Heading, Pitch and Roll, plus **Model Scale** (0.5 to 1.5; 1 sizes the model to 4.5 units, inside the sphere), **Model Bounce** (it bobs with the motion and the kick, replacing the three bars) and **Model Glow** (the halo, now 0.3 by default). The model keeps its own materials; Intensity at 0 hides it. The wordmark, edge and core palette colors are gone (the preset is revision 3, so saved Bass Sigil settings reset once).
- **What a model may contain.** Imported models may carry embedded textures (the import's limits: 8192 px a side, about 768 MiB decoded) and animations, which are kept in the file but not played. Models with a skeleton (skins) are refused with a message, because the scene bridge clones meshes per instance.
- **Texture tiers.** The scene keeps the decoded textures as saved and gives the materials smaller copies, sized to the tier's memory allowance: longest side at most 1024 px on **low**, 2048 px on **medium** and 4096 px on **high**, and the whole model's textures within about 20, 48 and 96 MB. Those allowances are what is left of the engine's 96, 160 and 256 MB GPU budgets after the render targets (measured 43, 64 and 95 MB at 1080p) and the scene. A model that fits keeps every map at the tier's side; one that does not has its largest maps (by memory) halved one at a time until it fits, so small maps stay sharp. Copies are made once per texture and size, halved step by step, and released when the model is replaced or the scene is disposed. This is what keeps the "running above its GPU memory budget because of loaded 3D assets" notice away: before the allowances a model with 4096 px maps (for example `pure_diamond.glb` and `demon_mask.glb`, about 257 MB each) was uploaded at full size on high. Measured in Chromium with those two files: 64, 32 and 16 MB on high, medium and low. Applying a tier takes 0.1 to 0.7 s once, when the model first appears or the tier changes.

## Glyph Sphere (Glyph Armillary), revision 3

Revision 3 replaces the static environment with a native module (`glyph-sphere-native`, `modules/Cinema2GlyphSphereNativeModule.ts` and `modules/glyphSphere/GlyphSphereScene.ts`). The asset, preset id and bloom/finish effect ids are unchanged.

**Scene.** The source mesh is drawn four times: a white core (2.65× scale, with a fresnel halo) and three bands (inner cyan, middle amber, outer violet), each clipped to a latitude slab by a per-band shader. Each band sits in its own ring frame: two metal rails, 32 index ticks per ring and four vertical joint bars, plus a beacon at each long tick. A gradient dome and two starfield layers sit behind; the starfield is 900 points on high, 450 on medium and none on low. The source model's three baked morph targets drive the core's breathing, with an offset per band.

**Motion.** A phase accumulates in beats from the shared beat clock, so the bands turn faster on a faster track, hold while paused and idle at the 120 BPM reference with no source. Bands turn in alternating directions, rock on their tilt, and the frames counter-turn. A bright pulse circles each tick ring; its per-instance colour is rewritten every frame.

**Controls.** Master Controls: BPM Sync, Intensity, Audio Reactivity, Auto Performance. Design: Core (Scale, Glow, Breath), Bands (Spin, Spread, Wobble, Lighting), Frames (Frame Glow, Index Ticks, Joint Lights), Camera (mode, motion, Kick Zoom, speed, manual heading/elevation/distance, field of view). Effects: Core Halo, Bloom, Starfield, Tone Finish. Palette: Background, Core, Inner/Middle/Outer Band, Frames.

**Camera.** Manual, Horizontal and Omnidirectional orbit variants; the 44 s omnidirectional path is tempo-synced and a kick zooms the field of view.

**Choreography.** Bass compresses the core, overall energy speeds the bands, highs lift the ticks, vocals ease the band light; kicks, snares and drops fire short accents. All scale with Audio Reactivity. With Auto Performance on, the Mood Conductor drives calm, momentum, energy, tension, build, peak and shock, and silence pulls the lighting controls to 10%. Lighting zeros stay authoritative.

**Verification.** `Cinema2GlyphSphere.test.ts` and `Cinema2GlyphSpherePerformance.test.ts` (including the shared mood-migration suite and the Auto Performance coverage test) pass. In headless Chromium with software WebGL the preset renders without errors on high and medium; a real PCM soundtrack through the production analyser pump moved the live targets (core scale 0.93–1.00, band spin 1.0–1.3, ticks to 1.2, frame glow to 1.14, core glow to 1.08). That run did not exercise snare or drop accents, and nothing here was measured on a real GPU.


### Paradox Weave text effects, transitions and score

**Text effects.** Each Pattern plays its own effect on the letters (`modules/crate/Cinema2ParallaxCipherTextEffects.ts`): Reading Line sweeps a light along the word, Open Gate parts the letters and leans them apart, Collapse draws them together and flares them on every beat, Counter Spiral winds neighbours against each other, Cascade runs a lifting ripple along the word, and Shatter spins every letter on its own axes each beat. Effects are shaped to settle between beats so the word still reads at the reveal hold. **Letter Motion** scales them; at 0 the word stays still and a line change is instant.

**Transitions.** When the next line takes over, the active Pattern's transition runs over four beats: the old line leaves letter by letter for two, the text swaps while it is hidden, and the new line arrives for two. Reading Line folds the letters like flap tiles, Open Gate slides them out to the sides, Collapse implodes the word, Counter Spiral turns each a half turn, Cascade drops the old line and lets the new one fall in from above, and Shatter throws the letters apart and pulls them back. With Auto Performance on, a drop also brings the other line on stage (at most once every eight beats).

**Score.** Like Bass Sigil, the kick, snare and drop each fire a cue on a hidden channel (`platePulse`, `letterRipple`, `dropRelease`) that the scene plays: the kick lifts, widens and lights the plate banks nearest the viewer first, the snare ripples the letters, and the drop throws the word, pushes the banks out and pops the weave. **Score Amount** (Design → Pattern, default 0.5) sets how much; zero turns the score off. Cues scale with Audio Reactivity. Auto Performance adds a director route on Letter Motion and mood drives on it (calm quietens the letters, a build, a driving passage and a peak agitate them, silence pulls them to 10%) on top of the existing build (Collapse), drop (Shatter) and mood-chosen patterns.

**Verification.** `Cinema2ParallaxCipherPreset.test.ts` covers the inputs, every pattern's effect and transition, line rotation, instant edits, Letter Motion 0, the score channels and the drop line change; the Auto Performance coverage and mood-migration suites pass. In headless Chromium (software WebGL) a two-line word was rendered through the real runtime and the transition was watched from PARADOX to CIPHER. Not exercised: the score cues with real audio, and the effects on a real GPU.


## Wireframe Portal (Corner Truss), revision 3

Revision 3 turns the static truss into a kinetic light rig with a native module (`wireframe-portal-native`, `modules/Cinema2WireframePortalNativeModule.ts`, `modules/wireframePortal/WireframePortalScene.ts` and `WireframePortalPatterns.ts`). The asset and preset id are unchanged; the effects are new (`wireframe-portal-focus`, `-bloom`, `-flares`, `-grade`), which is safe because the old preset had no controls and so no saved settings. The old shared crate presentation class is gone; Paradox Weave keeps its own module.

**The rig.** The 28 chamfered bays are steel groups that move as kinetic parts, each with a hot core. Twelve fixtures on the outermost bays throw beams (an additive cone with a lens glow, red, cold white and amber) through haze; the bars between bays are rebuilt every frame so they stretch with the bays. Two hard rim lights (cold white above left, red lower right) sweep slowly, with a low warm kick from the front. The background is near black with a cold haze band and dust (700 points on high, 350 on medium, none on low). The finish is shallow depth of field, bloom, anamorphic glare, a cold-shadow and warm-highlight grade, vignette and grain.

**Programs.** Six Patterns, each moving the bays, setting the cores and aiming the beams, all as functions of the beat position:
- **Lattice**: the structure breathes and rocks; the beams fan slowly like searchlights.
- **Extrude**: every beat the bays punch out along their axis and fall back; the beams flare open on the beat.
- **Rotor**: waves of bays turn a quarter turn once a bar; two counter-rotating crowns of beams sweep.
- **Fold**: the bays are drawn in and shudder with their cores ramping hotter; the beams close on the centre and tighten (the build).
- **Ripple**: a wave of height runs through the lattice; the beams rise and fall fixture to fixture.
- **Detonate**: once a bar the structure blows apart along random lines, spinning, and pulls back together; every beam fires out (the drop).

Pattern, Pattern Change and the Trigger work as in the other pattern presets; programs ease into each other over two beats.

**Music.** A kick writes the hidden `flash` channel (cores, rims and beams flare, scaled by Strobe) and a drop writes `blast` (every bay is thrown outward, the haze and red rim surge, and the beams chop four times a beat, scaled by Strobe). Bass pushes Kinetics, energy speeds the sweep, highs light the edge runs, snares flare the beams and rims, vocals ease the rims back; all scale with Audio Reactivity.

**Auto Performance.** The music chooses the program: a build puts Fold on stage, a drop puts Detonate on stage for eight beats, and between them the mood chooser picks (calm: Lattice or Ripple; driving: Rotor or Extrude; tense: Fold or Rotor; peak: Extrude or Detonate), never repeating the current program. Pattern Change and the Trigger become the music's choice while it is on, and the saved Pattern is never rewritten. The Mood Conductor also drives Kinetics, beams, strobe, haze, dust, rims, steel and the bloom, glare and focus effects, and silence pulls the lighting and motion to 10%.

**Camera.** Director (default) cuts an 84 s loop: a wide orbit, a swoop to the front, a flight down the middle column through four bays, a low orbit around the back, a flight along the row through five bays, and a rising orbit back to the start. Flythrough keeps to the two corridors. Horizontal turns around the truss and Manual places the camera. Each flight stops a few units past the last bay, and its view swings back toward the truss from the middle on, so the camera never looks into empty space (a test checks that the truss stays in view from every point outside it on both paths). Lenses, beams and cores fade out as the camera passes close to them, so none fills the screen. All are tempo-synced, and a kick zooms the field of view.

**Controls.** Master Controls: BPM Sync, Intensity, Audio Reactivity, Auto Performance. Design: Pattern (Pattern, Pattern Change, Trigger, Kinetics), Structure (Joint Heat, Edge Runs, Wayfinding Pins, Steel Roughness), Lighting (Beam Lighting, Rim Lighting, Ember Drift, Sweep Rate, Strobe), Camera. Effects: Haze, Dust, Bloom, Joint Glare, Depth Focus, Tone Finish. Palette: Background, Steel, Hot Joints, Edge Runs, Cold Rim, Red Rim.

**Verification.** `Cinema2WireframePortal.test.ts` and `Cinema2WireframePortalPerformance.test.ts` (including the shared mood-migration suite and the pattern Auto Performance tests) pass, and the Auto Performance coverage tests for this preset pass. In headless Chromium with software WebGL each program and the flythrough were rendered without errors and watched. Not done: a real-audio run, a real GPU check (the beams, twelve cone meshes, the per-frame bar rebuild, two spot lights and the depth-of-field pass are the likely costs), and a check that no rail clips the near plane during a Detonate or Extrude while flying through a bay.

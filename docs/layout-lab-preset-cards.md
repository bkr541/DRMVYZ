# Archived Layout Lab preset card studies

The Preset Cards tab was removed after its final 02 Matte Frame design became the [production poster style](react-preset-thumbnails.md). The notes below document earlier iterations of the studies.

The stable Preset cards tab (`layout-lab-tab-02`) contains twenty-one comparison rows. Renaming the tab preserves its content. All rows use the production 148.5px comparison width. Rows 01–05 use thumbnail headers with opaque captions underneath; their height grows with the name. Rows 06–10 retain the original Performance Pad studies at 148.5 × 69px.

Rows 01–05 use five different surfaces and frames:

- **01 Cinema classic:** full-width 16:9 thumbnail over a charcoal caption, with rounded corners and a fine separator.
- **02 Matte frame:** inset thumbnail inside a photographic mat, with a centered caption below.
- **03 Accent gallery:** full-width thumbnail, a preset-colored seam and a deep dark caption.
- **04 Light folio:** panoramic 2:1 thumbnail above a pale paper caption with dark text.
- **05 Studio panel:** recessed framed thumbnail over a slate caption with an inset color rail.

For every thumbnail, names are readable because the image and caption occupy separate normal-flow boxes. Caption backgrounds are fully opaque and independent of artwork; title colors are fixed high-contrast pairs. The shared preset-name role remains 12px. Names wrap without truncation or a line limit, including unbroken words, and the card grows to fit. Selection and favorite actions stay in the thumbnail area, outside the title.

Rows 01–05 validation: two focused popup tests and focused ESLint passed. Chromium checked all twenty cards, selection, favorites and keyboard activation. White, black and striped thumbnail replacements left title contrast unchanged (12.67:1 minimum across the five surfaces). A long name containing an unbroken word wrapped in full without overlapping the image or clipping. Reviewed captures: ignored `artifacts/layout-lab-preset-cards/rows-01-05-captioned.png` and `rows-01-05-contrast-stress.png`.

Every row compares **Reactor**, **Ancient Machine**, **CANOPY** and **Electric Storm**. The second and fourth slots use current production Cinema 2.0 presets to expose two-line title behavior. Long names wrap at word boundaries inside a consistent title measure; the full production name remains visible. Images are the production-renderer frames in `public/preset-artwork/`, generated by `scripts/generate-preset-artwork.mjs`.

Three independent asset-browser studies follow the original ten:

- **11 Asset library:** 16:9 preview above a solid caption area, two-line title space, engine metadata and a separate favorite action.
- **12 Compact library:** a thumbnail beside stacked title and metadata for dense browsing.
- **13 Immersive gallery:** full-card preview, an anchored contrast scrim, title, engine badge and floating favorite action.
- **14–16 Poster legibility:** the original production poster tile (artwork edge to edge, centred title in the shared preset-name role) with three overlay treatments. These legacy rows remain separate from the thumbnail-header designs in rows 01–05.
  - **14 Soft scrim:** a dark radial scrim behind the title that eases to nothing at the edges, so the picture still shows. Measured in Chromium (white text against the brightest pixel behind it) at least 9.2:1 on the four preset renders and on a pure-white thumbnail.
  - **15 Frosted band:** a full-width band behind the title that blurs and darkens only that strip, feathered at all four edges so it reads as soft glass, not a box. The blur removes bright detail (lightning, beams) behind the letters while the rest of the tile stays sharp.
  - **16 Outlined title:** no background at all; a dark outline is painted under the letters with a tight halo, as film subtitles do, so the whole picture stays visible.
  Rows 15 and 16 were checked by eye on the four presets, a pure-white thumbnail and bright stripes; they have no numeric contrast figure. (A first version of these two rows, a solid name plate and a solid caption bar, was replaced because it boxed the title in and hid the artwork.)

Rows 17–21 add five distinct browsing layouts at the same 148.5px comparison width:

- **17 Split aperture:** a circular scene window on the right, beside a solid left-aligned caption (100px tall).
- **18 Index ticket:** a numbered spine and perforated divider beside a scene preview and caption (138px tall).
- **19 Panorama dossier:** the title above a panoramic scene with a separate engine footer (132px tall).
- **20 Offset sleeve:** tilted, framed artwork with a layered backing above a solid caption (158px tall).
- **21 Lens badge:** a centered circular scene with a concentric frame above a stacked caption (158px tall).

These new studies follow the preview/title/metadata/secondary-action anatomy described in [Adobe Spectrum's card guidance](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/cards/), using the application's shared typography roles and focus colors. Their layout and surface rules are independent of the Performance Pad treatments.

Selection and favorites are independent per row and remain local to the study. No engine, audio or cast runtime is started. Card actions support keyboard focus and activation. All four cards stay in each comparison row; the surrounding Layout Lab canvas supports scrolling at narrow widths.

Rows 17–21 validation: five focused popup/tab tests and focused ESLint passed. Chromium verified all twenty new cards load artwork and retain the shared 12px title role without clipping; selection, independent favorites and keyboard activation passed for each layout. The visual review is saved under ignored `artifacts/layout-lab-preset-cards/five-new-studies.png`. Existing duplicate-key warnings from navigation mockups remain unrelated to these cards.

## Engine dropdown studies (tab 3, default name "Engine dropdown")

Five rows, each showing every production engine choice (`REACT_ENGINE_IDS`) as an option card at the exact production size: 330 × 84 px (the 354 px rail less its 12 px inset; card height 96 px less that inset), 1 px neutral border, 4 px corner, 6 px 12 px 4 px padding, with the production card contents (blueprint artwork, pixel label for PixGrid) and the shared engine eyebrow/label type roles. Rows stay inside the application's look (dark panel surfaces, the cyan primary, hairline borders) and differ in structure: Accent rail (left rail, small blueprint at right, index), Icon tile (framed blueprint chip, two-line text), Spotlight wash (edge-to-edge artwork under a primary wash, title at the foot), Drafting grid (full grid field with corner ticks) and Recessed well (darker inset surface, ghosted artwork, underline that fills on selection). Selection is local to a row and never changes the real engine. Sound Drawing XY has no blueprint artwork in production, so its art slot is empty in every row. Source: `layoutLab/EngineDropdownStudies.tsx` and `engineDropdownStudies.css`.

## Navigation icon studies (tab 4, default name "Nav bar icons")

Row 01 (Hairline duotone) opens with a live stage icon for React, showing an audio pulse and a visual spark. Its Lyric Manager icon uses a microphone with lyric lines, and its Media Manager icon adapts row 19’s four tiles for images, video, audio and fonts. React has its own Keep checkbox in this row.

The twenty study rows show six custom SVG path icons each. Most rows start with Performance Pads, followed by Presets Manager, Show Manager, Lyric Manager, Media Manager and Notifications. Every glyph is drawn on the navigation's 32 grid in `currentColor` plus one `--llni-accent` shape (the convention production's nav already uses), and each cell shows the artwork large and as the real collapsed-sidebar item (72 px column, 28 px icon, 3 px active edge, active glow). The systems differ in construction, not just stroke: Hairline duotone, Bold line, Solid glyph, Tinted fill, Technical cut, Plotter dash, Offset depth, Badge tile, Medallion and LED matrix, then five inspired by reference icon sets: Hex fragments (broken hexagon frames with accent segments), Offset duotone (slate outline with the accent shifted off the edge), Fade trail (strokes fading along their length with a ghost echo), Dark inlay (light outline around a dark tinted interior) and Flat geometry (stroke-free circles, bars and triangles). The last five follow a content brief: Pad console, Neon tube, Spec sheet, Solid stamp and Chip tags. Their Performance Pads follow production's pad surface (dark tile, 1px border, colour line, lit hit state), Presets show configuration (sliders, knobs, toggles, parameter tables), Show combines DJ lighting fixtures and beams with a timeline, Lyrics combine a mic, lyric lines and a note, and Media shows image, video, audio and font types. After the first review, the 16 icons marked Keep were frozen and the other 104 were redrawn from scratch to the content brief (production-pad surface, configuration, DJ lighting plus timeline, mic with lyrics and a note, several media types); keep ids are unchanged, so the saved Keep marks still apply to the same 16 icons. Row 09 (Medallion) supplied the production navigation artwork. The lab still shows the Medallion study, and the live rail uses supplied assets for React, Preset Manager and Lyric Manager. Notifications has its own icon. Below the twenty rows, a Performance Pads options row (`NAV_PAD_OPTIONS`) shows five candidate symbols inside the production Medallion ring at the production size, each with its own Keep checkbox, followed by a React options row (`NAV_REACT_OPTIONS`) with five candidate symbols for the React (home) item, then two more React rows drawn from what React is in the app: a live-stage row (monitor, stage over pads, output cast, engine stack, stage over dock) and an audio-reaction row (wave to shape, beat burst, spectrum frame, scope dial, live indicator), then two rows from the engine functionality in `reactEngineCatalog.ts`: engine marks (Cinema 2.0, Sound Drawing, CANVAS, LaserDMX, PixGrid) and the engine system (hub of seven, switcher, selector, one in many looks, rack). Each icon has a Keep checkbox (saved in the popup) to mark which to keep; unchecked ones are to be redone. The icon library is `layoutLab/navIconSystems.tsx` (`NavIconArtwork`); the row styling is `navIconStudies.css`.
